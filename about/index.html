Long before orchestrating language models or vector clusters, my foundation was forged in deterministic reasoning: chess calculation, mathematical problem-solving, and stripping machines down to bare metal. When you diagnose hardware at the component level, abstractions cannot hide a fault — either the signal crosses the bus and the system boots, or it does not.

While completing my Technical Degree in Computer Science at Cotemig (2018 – 2020) in Belo Horizonte, I worked as an Apprentice — Computer Maintenance (Jan 2019 – Jan 2020), performing desktop hardware diagnosis, component-level repair, and preventative maintenance.

From there, I stepped into enterprise operations as an IT Technician N2 at Feluma (Feb 2020 – Jan 2021), delivering local and field-based technical support and resolving workstation, connectivity, and infrastructure issues within N2 scope. Troubleshooting physical links and OS states under real user pressure built the instinct that still governs my AI engineering today: isolate variables, verify the physical and network layer first, and never guess when you can measure.

Act III · Operating System & Discipline Jeet Kune Do · MUVA · PUC Minas

Philosophy of Execution: Jeet Kune Do (JKD) & MUVA

High-reliability engineering is not an accident of tooling; it is the outward expression of an internal operating system. Two core principles govern how I study, train, and architect production software: Jeet Kune Do (JKD) and MUVA (Smallest Viable Unit of Action).

From Jeet Kune Do — absorb what is useful, reject what is useless, add what is essentially your own — comes radical architectural economy. In AI infrastructure, that means refusing framework bloat, stripping away decorative layers that obscure latency or token cost, and choosing boring, inspectable primitives (Linux, systemd, WireGuard, nginx, deterministic state machines) over fragile abstractions.

From MUVA comes momentum through bounded, verifiable execution: decomposing complex multi-agent platforms or daily academic and physical commitments into the smallest self-contained unit that can be built, tested, and verified end to end today. This same discipline drives my daily physical training, my degree in Systems Analysis and Development at PUC Minas (Jan 2026 – Jun 2028, in progress), and my ongoing preparation for the CKA — Certified Kubernetes Administrator examination (in progress).

Act IV · Subagentic RAG & LLMOps 2026 – Present · MaxStartup & Mother / Hermes

Autonomous Architecture: MaxStartup & Mother / Hermes

Every lesson from bare metal, NOC monitoring, observability automation, and JKD/MUVA converged when I transitioned full-force into production AI engineering. During my 6 months engagement as Senior AI Engineer (Contract) at MaxStartup (Feb 2026 – Jul 2026), I was the Sole architect and developer of the company's AI integrations and automations — building a multi-agent knowledge system combining knowledge management by trust level, RAG, and autonomous workflows with LangGraph, Crawl4AI, LangFuse, and Qdrant.

In parallel, I architected and operate Mother / Hermes, a self-hosted subagentic RAG platform built end to end around operational sovereignty and cost determinism:

• Multi-Domain Vector Isolation: Self-hosted Qdrant deployment using a Multi-Domain collection-per-domain architecture where the vector index is treated as derived, rebuildable state backed by a filesystem-first knowledge hierarchy (raw, reviewed, and canonical trust tiers).
• Zero Per-Query Embedding Cost: Local Ollama embeddings (nomic-embed-text, 768d) eliminating external embedding API latency and billing.
• End-to-End Observability & Governance: A Self-hosted LangFuse stack (web, worker, ClickHouse, Postgres, MinIO, and Redis) paired with bounded subagent delegation (centralized orchestration, maximum spawn depth of one, isolated child contexts, fail-closed design), LlamaIndex, MCP protocols, and public architectural documentation in tanya-showcase/ behind a WireGuard-restricted nginx boundary.
